Reliability Field Guide

Tokens, Quotas & Cost

How tokens connect speed, cost, and capacity, and how to stop quota throttling, the most common AI outage, before it happens.

Use This When

The assistant is failing with throttling errors, a launch is coming, the cloud bill is questioned, or someone proposes a longer prompt or a bigger model. Tokens connect reliability, speed, and cost, and quotas are the most common cause of AI outages.

Tokens in Plain Terms

  • A token is a chunk of text, roughly ¾ of a word. "Reliability engineering" is about 3–4 tokens.
  • Input tokens are everything sent to the model: instructions, conversation history, retrieved documents, and the user's question.
  • Output tokens are the answer.
  • Providers charge per token (output usually costs more), limit you per token, and take longer for more tokens.

In other words, a prompt that grows from 2,000 to 6,000 tokens makes every request slower, costlier, and three times closer to your quota.

Quotas and Throttling

  • Tokens per minute (TPM) and requests per minute (RPM) are the usual limits on a model deployment.
  • Exceed them and the provider returns HTTP 429 (Too Many Requests), which is throttling. Users see errors or long waits as the client retries.
  • Retries without backoff make throttling worse: every failed call is retried immediately, adding load (a "retry storm").
  • Quotas are usually per region and per deployment, so traffic can be spread across them. Confirm how yours is set up.

Pay-as-you-go (standard)Pay per token, within shared capacity and quota limitsFlexible and cheap at low volume. Latency and throttling can vary at peak.
Provisioned (reserved) throughputReserve dedicated model capacity for a fixed priceMore predictable latency and capacity. You pay for it whether you use it or not.

The decision between them is a business one. Bring the throttling rate, TTFT at peak, and the cost per 1,000 requests.

Prompt-Length Governance

  • Track input tokens per request as a metric on the scorecard. Any prompt change that raises it by more than an agreed amount (for example, 20%) needs a review.
  • Cap conversation history and retrieved context. More context isn't always better answers, but it's always more tokens.
  • Set a maximum output length per journey.
  • Use caching where it applies: repeated instructions, common answers. Confirm what your provider and orchestration support.

Cost per Request

Track cost per 1,000 requests and cost per active user monthly: model spend from Azure Cost Management, divided by request volume from Application Insights (or active users from Pendo). Flag any change that moves it by more than 10%. See Cost per Request.

Alerting

What Done Looks Like

  • An alert at 80% of TPM or RPM quota, per deployment, at low urgency, so someone acts before users notice.
  • A paging alert when the throttling rate goes above 1% for 5 minutes, routed to PagerDuty.
  • Retries use exponential backoff with jitter, with a capped retry count.
  • Input tokens per request, output tokens per request, and cost per 1,000 requests are on the scorecard.
  • Quota headroom is checked before every launch, along with load testing. See Capacity.

Example in Practice

A new feature adds the user's last 20 messages to every prompt, and input tokens per request triple. Nothing breaks at 10 a.m. At the 2 p.m. peak, the deployment hits its TPM quota and 18% of calls get 429s. The 80% quota alert would have fired at 1:20 p.m. That's 40 minutes to shift traffic to a second deployment, or turn the feature off with a flag.
