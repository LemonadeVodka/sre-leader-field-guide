Wins & Evidence Log

Use This When

Something went right because of you or your team: a save, a decision, a fix, a runbook that finally works. Log it the same day, while you still remember the numbers.

Log a Win

Why This Matters More for You

Engineers have commits and designs to show for their work. Your work, like a fire that didn't spread, a pod that finally owned its alert, or an offshore team that stopped mis-escalating, leaves no trace unless you write it down. Without a record, people who don't see the work will judge you on how you come across.

Example in Practice

Weak entry: "Helped with alerts."

Strong entry: "Got the Assistant pod to own the timeout alert (it paged 40 times a week). Agreed on a fix date in pod sync, followed up twice. Now 3 pages a week. That's about 6 on-call hours a week back. Credit: Priya (fix), Sam (data)."

In other words: what changed, what it was worth, and who did it. That third part matters. Crediting your team by name builds trust with them, and shows leadership you're the one who makes things happen.

What Actually Happens
You forget to log wins during firefighting weeks, which are exactly the weeks with the best stories.
Counter: Friday's checklist on Today includes "log at least one win." On Fire Days, a single line is enough. Fill in the impact later.
Someone else presents your team's win as theirs.
Counter: a dated entry with evidence is your record. Mention the win in your weekly update first, with names, so the story is told your way before anyone else tells it.
At review time, your manager remembers the last two weeks and the one bad incident.
Counter: "Copy This View as Bullets" for the quarter, and send it before your review is written, not after.
