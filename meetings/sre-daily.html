SRE Leader's Field Guide

SRE Daily

A 30-minute weekday meeting for decisions, not status. It covers operations, progress on reliability projects, what's shipping soon, learning, and how the team is doing.

Quick Card: Run Today's SRE Daily

  1. Wins (2 min): one or two specific shout-outs, by name.
  2. Operations (10 min): last 24 hours, health verdict, NOC handoff, what's stuck.
  3. Engineering (7 min): walk the board, not the people: moved, next, blocked.
  4. Changes and rotating segment (8 min): the next 48 hours of changes, then the day's segment.
  5. Asks and recap (3 min): who needs help, parking lot, actions read back.

Purpose

Start every day with one shared picture: operations (what happened, are we healthy, what's stuck), engineering progress on reliability work, what's coming, and how the team is doing. It's for decisions and unblocking, not a round-robin status read-out.

Who's in the Room

  • The SRE team, including whoever is on call.
  • The NOC shift lead, for the 10-minute operations block only, if time zones allow. If they can't attend, use their written handoff.
  • Project owners speak in the engineering block, for their item only.
  • A facilitator who rotates weekly. You don't have to run it every day, and having others facilitate builds the team and frees you to listen.
  • You: listen for what's stuck, take the blockers, recognize good work, and protect focus time.

Before the Meeting

  • On-call posts three lines in the team channel 10 minutes before: what paged, what's still open, and anything weird.
  • Project owners update the reliability project board (SLOs, automation, observability, toil) before the meeting, not during it.
  • One person checks the change calendar for the next 48 hours: deploys, prompt or model changes, launches. This rotates with the facilitator.
  • The learning-share rota is published a week ahead, so Tuesday's and Thursday's presenters can prepare 4 minutes.
  • You: read the on-call note and the NOC handoff, and note one win to call out.

Fire-Day Rule

On a Sev 1 or Sev 2 day, operations takes the time it needs. The engineering walk and the rotating segment shrink, or move to tomorrow. The 2-minute wins block always stays. It costs almost nothing, and it's what keeps the team going through a bad week.

Questions to Ask

Bring three. Mix operations with one engineering or team question. Each question has a follow-up for a weak answer, and coaching notes on what good sounds like.

Example in Practice

Minute 1, wins. You: "Shout-out to Ana. Her runbook rewrite meant the NOC handled last night's queue alert alone, without paging anyone." The NOC lead on the call hears it too.

Minute 14, engineering walk. The board shows the "auto-restart for the stuck queue worker" automation as blocked for 6 days. You: "What's it waiting on?" It needs a service account from the platform team. You: "That one's mine. I'll ask their manager today." It ships two days later and removes about 4 pages a week.

Thursday's learning share. An engineer spends 4 minutes on how last week's model-provider throttling showed up in Application Insights before anything paged. The NOC lead asks for screenshots for the runbook. Two weeks later, the NOC spots the same pattern early and escalates in 5 minutes. In other words, the non-ops blocks prevented incidents, not just reported on them.

What Actually Happens

Operations swallows the whole meeting every day.
Counter: the facilitator keeps time out loud ("2 minutes left on ops"). Anything longer goes to the parking lot. If it's a real fire day, use the fire-day rule on purpose, not by accident.
The project walk turns into status theatre.
Counter: walk the board, and only discuss items that moved, changed date, or are blocked. "No change" items get skipped.
Learning shares get skipped "just this week," every week.
Counter: publish the rota, keep it to 4 minutes, and allow small topics: one graph, one query, one lesson from an RCA.
Recognition feels forced or generic.
Counter: be specific: who, what they did, and what it made possible. "Great job, team" doesn't count. Rotate who gives the shout-out.
It becomes a round-robin where everyone reads their ticket list.
Counter: go by agenda block, not by person. If someone has nothing to add, they say nothing, and that's a good daily.
The NOC handoff gets skipped when the meeting runs long.
Counter: it sits inside the operations block, early on purpose. Skipping it tells the offshore team their work doesn't matter.

Rules for Every Recurring Meeting

  • Same format every time. People prepare for a format they can predict.
  • Hard stop at 30 minutes. End early if you're done. A meeting that respects time gets attended.
  • Anything over 2 minutes goes to the parking lot, with two named people to take it right after.
  • No action without an owner, a date, and what "done" means.
  • A three-line written recap within the hour. It reaches people who missed it, and it becomes the record.
