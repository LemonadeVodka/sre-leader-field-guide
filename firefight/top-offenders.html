Top Offenders Board

Use This When

The team is firefighting constantly and it all blurs together. In most unstable systems, a handful of recurring issues cause most of the pages. This board makes those few visible, puts a cost on each one, and gives each one an owner.

Add or Update an Offender

Pain score = pages per week + 2 × engineer hours + customer impact (low 5, medium 15, high 30). It's rough on purpose, because it's for ranking, not accounting.

Closed

Do This
  1. Start rough. Ask on-call: "What paged most this week, and roughly how long did it eat?" Five entries is enough to start.
  2. Update weekly. Monday's checklist on Today prompts you. Counts from the paging tool are better than guesses, but guesses are better than nothing.
  3. Get #1 owned. Not "acknowledged." Owned by a person, with a date. Use Make the Case to draft the message.
  4. Celebrate closures. When one is fixed, log the before and after numbers as a win.
Example in Practice

On-call has been "busy" for months. Nobody can say with what. You spend 20 minutes with the on-call engineer and log five issues. One, assistant timeouts at the afternoon peak, is 31 pages and about 6 engineer hours a week by itself. That's 15% of one engineer, every week, on one issue.

You click Make the Case, tweak the text, and send it to the Assistant pod's manager. You don't send it to the engineers, and you don't send it in a public channel. Instead of "SRE is complaining again," the conversation becomes "this costs us about 26 hours a month. Who owns it?"

What Actually Happens
The pod says, "That's not our code. It's the model provider."
Counter: "OK. Who on your side owns the relationship and the fallback? The provider failing is the cause. Us having no fallback is the problem." Ownership of the impact doesn't move just because the cause is outside.
Someone disputes your numbers to avoid the conversation.
Counter: "Happy to use your numbers. What do you see?" Then move on to the owner question. Arguing about whether it's 25 or 31 pages is a delay tactic.
Leadership looks at the board and asks why SRE hasn't fixed these.
Counter: be ready with the line. "Central SRE keeps them from becoming outages. The fixes live in pod code we don't own. The board shows who does." Then show the "no owner" count.
It gets "owned," and then the status doesn't move for a month.
Counter: put the fix in the Commitment Tracker with a due date. Two slips and it goes into your monthly reliability review, with the pod named.
