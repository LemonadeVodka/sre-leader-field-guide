RCA Day One: What to Do Today

The first hour after an incident, the days to the review, making actions stick, a 30-day starter plan, what to look for by incident type, and copy-ready messages.

Use This When

An incident just closed and needs an RCA, or you're starting an RCA practice from nothing. This page is the "what do I do and send today" companion to RCA the SRE Way (the thinking) and Running an RCA (the method).

The First Hour After an Incident Closes

  • Name the author. Usually the incident lead or an engineer from the owning pod. Not you.
  • Book the review now: draft in 3 working days, review in 5. Send the invite below.
  • Grab the evidence before it's gone (retention, dashboard time ranges, and memories all fade):
    • Export the PagerDuty incident timeline: alerts, pages, acknowledgements, escalations.
    • Screenshot or save links to the Grafana panels for the incident window, with the time range fixed, not "last 6 hours."
    • Save the Application Insights / Log Analytics query links that showed the failures, plus 2–3 example request or trace IDs.
    • Note the time range to check in the Azure Activity Log and the deploy history (an hour before impact through to resolution).
    • Save or export the incident channel, and any bridge notes.
  • Send the "assigning the RCA" message below, so the author has everything in one place.

Days 1–3: Getting the Draft Right

Have a 15-minute check-in with the author halfway through the draft. Ask:

  1. "Where did each time on the timeline come from?"
  2. "What's the trigger, and what made us vulnerable to it?"
  3. "Which of the four defenses failed: prevent, detect, mitigate, respond?"
  4. "What's the one action you'd do if you could only do one?"
  5. "Who outside the team needs to read this?"

If the draft comes back weak, send the feedback reply from Judging an RCA. It's specific and doesn't need technical depth.

Review Day

  • Run the 45-minute agenda, and read the blameless opening aloud.
  • Invite the person closest to the first alert, often a NOC analyst, to walk through the first 15 minutes.
  • Leave with 3–5 ranked actions, each with a person, a date, and what "done" means.
  • Within the hour, send the "RCA complete" share-out below.

After: Making Actions Actually Happen

  • Where actions live: your team's work tracker, labelled rca-action, with due dates and the RCA linked. Not in the RCA document alone, where they'll be forgotten.
  • Monthly review: filter on rca-action in the monthly reliability review. Anything overdue gets a decision: help, rescope, or escalate.
  • Nudge slipping actions with the message below. Two slips means it's a priority conflict for the pod's manager, not a motivation problem.
  • Verify the top action. Prove it reduces the risk with a drill, a test, or by watching the metric that failed. Then log it as a win.

The One-Page RCA Cheat Sheet

Print this section, or keep it open in a tab

The 8 steps: 1. Does it need an RCA? · 2. Timeline from evidence · 3. Impact in user terms · 4. Trigger vs conditions · 5. Four defenses · 6. 5 Whys (branched) · 7. 3–5 ranked actions · 8. Review and follow-through.

The four defenses: Prevent (why could it happen?) · Detect (why didn't we know sooner?) · Mitigate (why did it hurt this much?) · Respond (why did humans take this long?).

Six timestamps: impact started · detected · acknowledged · escalated · mitigated · resolved. Gaps to compare: time to detect, and time from detect to mitigate.

Red flags: "human error" as the root cause · "be more careful" · 15 unranked actions · no detection action · owners are teams · "investigate further" · timeline with no sources · treated as first-time when it isn't.

Your questions: "What made that possible?" · "What would have told us 10 minutes sooner?" · "If we only did one action, which?" · "Has this happened before?"

What Actually Happens

The evidence is gone by the time the author starts.
Counter: the first-hour checklist exists for this reason. Do the evidence grab yourself if you have to. It doesn't need technical skill.
Everyone agrees to the RCA, then the next incident eats the week.
Counter: the review is booked before anyone leaves the incident. Moving it requires a new date, not "later."
Actions live in the RCA document and die there.
Counter: the rca-action label and the monthly review are what keep them alive.
