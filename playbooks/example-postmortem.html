Wednesday to Thursday Morning: PREP

You own postmortems now, so the meeting starts the morning after the incident. Full method and every message: PREP → SAFER → CLOSE.

PWed 09:00Pick

Sev 2, customer-facing: it qualifies. You'd like Noor's teammate to write the RCA, but Sam insists on writing it himself. You agree; arguing about authorship on day one isn't worth it. You facilitate. Booked for Thursday 14:00.

The nuance: the person most likely to be defensive is now the author. You'll handle that in the Evidence step, not by overruling him.

RWed 09:30Request

You send the calendar invite with the SAFER agenda, ask Sam for a draft by Wednesday 17:00, and ask Asha for the NOC timeline from tool timestamps. Jordan declines because of the launch; Dee will come instead.

The ask to Asha (NOC shift lead)
Hi Asha, for Thursday's postmortem on the assistant timeouts, can you send me the NOC timeline by Wednesday?
Please use timestamps from PagerDuty and chat, not memory: when the first alert fired and where it went, when the NOC noticed, and each escalation (who, how, when acknowledged).
Please join with Ravi if you can. It's blameless; we want your view.

EWed 17:40Evidence

Sam's draft arrives 40 minutes late. It says: "Root cause: model provider throttling. Contributing factors: none." That's defensive and incomplete. If you accept it, nothing on your side or Sam's changes.

Your comment on the draft (to Sam)
Thanks for turning this around fast. Two asks before Thursday:
1. Can we add the 21:10 retry change as a contributing factor? Not to blame anyone; it turned a provider problem into a bigger one, and that's the part we can actually change.
2. Can we add the timeline gap between 21:33 and 21:46 (Slack post, not a page)? That one's partly on SRE; our runbook didn't have platform's pager either.

Sam replies: "Fine, but the root cause is still the provider." You let that go for now; the meeting will sort it out with evidence.

S14:00Set the rules

You: "Thanks for coming. Three rules. One: blameless. We're looking at how our systems let this happen, not who. Everyone did what made sense with what they knew. Two: facts first; if we're not sure, we say so. Three: we leave with a few actions, each with one owner, a date, and what done means. If anyone hears blame, including from me, call it out."

Why say it every time: Noor and Ravi are the two people most likely to stay quiet. The rules are mostly for them.

A14:03Agree the impact

Sam: "I'd push back on '30%.' It was only EU."

You: "Fair. Marco, what do the numbers say?"

Marco: "EU peaked at 34% failing. Across all regions it was 18% over 80 minutes, about 41,000 failed requests. Support logged 37 tickets."

You: "So: 18% overall, 34% in EU at peak, 80 minutes, and we found it ourselves, 6 minutes after it started. Everyone OK with that?"

The nuance: Sam was partly right. "30%" was the EU number, said under pressure on the bridge. Correcting your own side's numbers in front of him buys you credibility for the harder conversation later.

F14:06Follow the timeline

Asha: "The first alert fired at 21:25, but into the Assistant pod's alert channel, which the NOC doesn't watch. We saw a gateway alert at 21:31 and opened the incident."

You: "Ravi, you were on shift. What did you see, and what did the runbook tell you to do?"

Ravi (after a pause): "I saw gateway errors. The runbook said page SRE, so I did. Platform wasn't in the runbook, so I posted in their channel at 21:33."

You: "Thank you. That's useful. And that's on SRE as much as anyone; our own runbook didn't have platform's pager either."

Owning your team's gap, out loud, makes it much easier for Sam to own his.

E14:21Explain what let it happen

Sam: "Honestly, the NOC should have caught this at 21:25."

You: "Part of that's true; there's a 6-minute gap between the alert and the incident. Let's look at what made it easy to miss. Where did that alert go?"

Asha: "A channel we don't have access to."

Sam: "OK. And Noor should have load-tested the retry change."

You: "If Noor had load-tested, what test would have shown the provider throttling us at peak? Do we have one?"

Noor: "No. Our review checklist doesn't ask about what happens when a dependency is slow. Nobody's does."

You: "Then it's not one person's mistake, it's a missing check. Let's write that down."

The factors on the whiteboard:

  1. The provider throttled our EU quota, and nobody was watching quota usage.
  2. The retry change multiplied load on a struggling dependency. The review had no blast-radius question.
  3. The first alert went to a channel the NOC can't see.
  4. Neither the NOC's nor SRE's runbook had platform's pager, and platform's escalation policy had one person.
  5. Failover worked, but needed a manager's approval at 22:36 because it was never pre-approved.

LFri 09:00Log

The rule: an action only counts if its owner said yes in the room, or their manager said yes in writing. Dee and Sam said yes; Jordan didn't, so failover isn't an action.

E30 days laterEnd it properly

What I'd Watch Next Time

Honest Notes

  • Sam's defensiveness wasn't really about the provider. His pod gets blamed a lot. A 10-minute chat with him on Wednesday, not just a document comment, would have made the meeting easier.
  • I nearly let "human error" slide because Noor wasn't arguing. Silence isn't agreement.
  • Ravi and Asha joined at their 6 a.m. Next time, rotate the meeting time or record it.
  • Two of four actions belong to people who don't report to me. I can't hold them accountable; I can only make progress visible. That's what Monday's sweep is for.
