Risk Register: When Nobody Will Fix It

You can't make another team care. You can make the risk visible, get someone with authority to own the decision, and protect what's inside your control.

Quick Card: When Nobody Will Fix It

  1. Ask why it isn't a priority. They may have a real reason.
  2. Write the risk as "if X, then users get Y, because Z," with evidence.
  3. Name the decision owner: the person with authority, not the team that's busy.
  4. Ask for a decision: fix, fund, or accept until a date. Confirm it in writing.
  5. Mitigate what's yours and review monthly. When it recurs, reopen it with the evidence.

You can't make people care. You can make the risk visible and make someone own the decision.

Use This When

A fix matters, but the team that owns it won't or can't do it, and your central SRE team has no authority to make it happen. See it play out in Wednesday: Nobody Will Fix It.

The "They Don't Care" Ladder

Go up one step at a time. Most risks get resolved by step 3 or 4. The register is step 5, not step 1.

StepWhat you doWhat you say
1. Ask whyFind out what's really in the way"Help me understand what's stopping this?"
2. Their languageConnect it to their pain: on-call, their SLO, their launch, cost, complaints"If this happens during your launch, it's your launch that looks broken."
3. Offer helpShrink their part: your team does the runbook, the analysis, the draft"What if we do the write-up and you just review?"
4. Manager to managerAgree who actually owns the decision"I think this is your director's call, not yours or mine."
5. Decision requestWrite it up; ask the person with authority to choose"Fix, fund, or accept until [date]. Any is fine; I just want a decision."
6. Mitigate yoursReduce the damage inside your own area"Accepted doesn't mean ignored. Here's what we'll do meanwhile."
7. Review and reopenMonthly review; reopen with evidence when it recurs"It happened again. Here's what the mitigation did, and what it still costs."

How to Write a Risk

WeakStrong
"Platform is unreliable.""If the model provider throttles a region at peak, up to a third of assistant requests in that region fail until a manager approves failover, because failover isn't pre-approved."
"We need more monitoring.""If quota usage hits 100%, we find out from customers, because nothing alerts on quota."
"NOC coverage is bad.""Between 22:00 and 06:00, incidents wait for the NOC to reach SRE by phone, because we have no overnight SRE on call. Last month that averaged 25 minutes."

The pattern: if [trigger], then [what users experience], because [the missing control]. Then evidence: dates and incidents, not opinions.

Always Inside Your Control

Even when nobody will fix it, you can always:

  • Detect it earlier: an alert that reaches the NOC and the bridge, not just the owning team.
  • Shorten the response: a runbook step that pages the owner and their manager together, with an escalation packet.
  • Pre-write the comms: the exec update and customer-facing wording, ready to send.
  • Count it: every occurrence, with minutes of impact. The count is what eventually gets it funded.

What Actually Happens

Honest Notes

  • Written risk acceptance annoys people. That's partly the point: it makes a quiet "no" into a visible decision. Pre-wire it every time, and let the team lead edit the wording.
  • Never use the register as a weapon. If it reads like "here's everything platform failed at," it'll be ignored, and you'll lose allies. Describe systems, not people.
  • Some execs won't reply. Write "decision requested [date], pending." That's also a record.
  • Register fatigue is real. Thirty entries nobody reads protect no one. Keep a top five visible.
  • Some risks are yours to accept, like gaps in your own team's coverage. Register them too; it keeps the register fair.
  • For the political side, see When It's Used on You and Political Playbooks.
