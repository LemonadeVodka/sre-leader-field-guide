SRE Leader's Field Guide

Meeting Starter

Set an agenda in 60 seconds, open with ground rules people respect, and hold the room so every meeting ends with decisions and owners.

Quick Card: Open Any Meeting in 60 Seconds

  1. Purpose: "We're here to [decide / unblock / review] [X]."
  2. Time: "We have [30] minutes. I'll keep us to time."
  3. Ground rules: pick the set for this meeting below and say it out loud.
  4. Ask: "Anything that must be covered today?" Park the rest.
  5. Close at minus 5 minutes: "What did we decide, who owns what, by when?"

Agenda in 60 Seconds

Fill in four lines and you have an agenda people respect. Put the decision first: if there's no decision or unblock needed, it may be an email.

60-second agenda
[Meeting name]: [date], [length] min
Purpose:   [one line: what this meeting is for]
Decision:  [the one decision or unblock we need today]

[N] min   [Block 1: context, the shortest possible]
[N] min   [Block 2: discussion of options]
[N] min   [Block 3: decide, owners, dates]

Notes and owners: [name]   Parking lot: end of notes

Rule of thumb for minutes: about 20% context, 50% discussion, 30% decisions and owners. For 30 minutes that's 5, 15, and 10.

Ready-made agendas, already in minutes: SRE Daily (30) · Performance Engineering (30) · Postmortem (45) · Task kickoff (15) · Vendor demo (45) · Weekly status sweep (10)

Ground Rules to Say Out Loud

Ground rules work when you say them at the start, every time, in the same words. Copy the set for your meeting.

Postmortem ground rules
Before we start, three rules:
1. Blameless. We're looking at how our system let this happen, not who did it.
   Everyone did what made sense with what they knew at the time.
2. Facts first. If we're not sure, we say "not sure" and someone checks.
3. We leave with a few actions, each with one owner, a date, and what done means.
If anyone hears blame, including from me, say so.
Incident bridge ground rules
Quick rules for this bridge:
1. [Name] is incident commander. The IC decides; everyone else advises.
2. One conversation at a time. Side discussions go to a thread.
3. Say what you're doing before you change anything in production.
4. I'm handling exec and stakeholder updates, every 15 minutes.
SRE Daily ground rules
Same rules as always:
1. Decisions and blockers, not status. Status lives in the board.
2. Anything over 2 minutes goes to the parking lot or a follow-up.
3. We end on time, or early.
Performance Engineering ground rules
Ground rules for today:
1. We start with the user-facing number, against target.
2. Every regression gets a suspected cause and an owner before we move on.
3. We leave with one owned fix, not a list of "we should."
Vendor demo ground rules (say to the vendor)
Thanks for coming. So we use the time well:
1. We'll start with our problem and criteria, 5 minutes.
2. Please demo on our scenario, not the standard demo.
3. We'll save 10 minutes to see failure cases and 10 for questions on fit, cost, and risk.

Holding the Room

These lines let you steer without sounding defensive. Say them calmly, then move on.

WhenSay
Someone is rambling"Let me pause you there. What's the one thing you need from this group?"
A tangent takes over"That's important. Let's park it and give it proper time. Who owns following up?"
Someone quiet knows more"[Name], you've been close to this. What are we missing?"
Blame starts"Let's look at what the system allowed, not who did it. What made that easy to get wrong?"
Nobody will decide"Who's the decision-maker here? If not today, what do you need, and by when?"
You don't understand the detail"Help me with the impact: what does this mean for users, and what do you need from me?"
Running out of time"We've got 5 minutes. Let's capture decisions and owners now."
A senior person derails"Great point. I want to give it the time it deserves. Can we take it as the first item next time?"

Questions to Ask

Example in Practice

A Postmortem That Started Going Sideways

Ten minutes into a postmortem, a pod lead says "the NOC should have caught this." The NOC analyst goes quiet. You say: "Let's look at what the system allowed, not who did it. What would have made this easy to catch?" The answer: the alert fired into a channel nobody watches overnight. That becomes the top action, owned by the pod lead, with a date.

Because you said the ground rules at the start, steering back felt fair, not personal.

What Actually Happens

  • Ground rules feel awkward the first three times. By the fourth, people expect them and start enforcing them for you.
  • Someone senior will test the rules. Hold the line politely once, in the room. Then talk to them afterwards.
  • Agendas without a decision line drift into status. If you can't write the decision line, ask if the meeting is needed.
  • Notes that come a day late don't get read. Post within the hour, even if they're rough.
