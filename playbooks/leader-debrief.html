After the Incident: Your 5-Minute Leader Debrief

The postmortem improves the system. This improves you. Five questions, same day, after every incident you're part of.

Quick Card: Five Minutes, Same Day

  1. Did I get a leader named fast, and was it someone other than me?
  2. Was the impact clear by minute 10?
  3. Did updates go out on time, and did I protect the engineers from exec heat?
  4. Did I escalate on a clock, not a feeling?
  5. What's one thing I'll do differently on the next bridge?

Why This Matters

The postmortem improves the system. This debrief improves you. The best incident leaders spend five minutes after every incident asking how they led, not just what broke. Do it the same day, alone, while it's fresh. Nobody else needs to see it.

Write It Down

Keep these in one running note. After five incidents, read them back: the patterns tell you exactly what to work on.

Leader debrief note
Incident: [title], [date], Sev [x]
Minutes to a named leader: [n]   Leader: [me / name]
Impact clear by minute 10: [yes / no]
Mitigation tried by minute 30: [yes / no]
Longest unanswered wait: [n] minutes, on [who]
Updates on schedule: [yes / no]
Work I took that wasn't mine: [none / what]
What went well: [one thing]
One thing to do differently next time: [one specific behavior]

What Your Notes Will Tell You

If you keep seeing…Work on
Slow to name a leaderSay the IC line in your first 60 seconds, every time, even if it feels awkward.
Long waits on other teamsAgree escalation clocks with pod leads in daylight. See the stuck clock.
Mitigation came lateGet pre-approved levers written down for your top services.
You ended up doing the workThe "own decisions, not work" lines. Ask "who owns the work?" out loud.
Exec pressure on engineersMove execs to a side thread in the first minute they join, with a timed update.
Couldn't tell where it was slowLearn the Request Map and get the missing timing points measured.

What Actually Happens

  • You'll skip it after a bad night. That's when it's most useful. Five minutes, even rough.
  • You'll be harder on yourself than anyone else would be. Write one thing that went well, every time.
  • After a month, your notes become your best coaching material for the SREs and NOC leads you're developing into incident leaders.
