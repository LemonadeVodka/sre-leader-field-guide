SRE Leader's Field Guide

Running a Task or Small Project

Kick off, keep moving, and close out a task that has engineers owning action items, without becoming the project manager.

Quick Card: Start, Run, and Finish a Task

  1. Kickoff (15 min): the problem, what done means, one owner per action, the first checkpoint.
  2. Write it down: the one-page task brief, posted in the channel.
  3. During: a 4-line update every 2 to 3 days. Ask for date confidence, 1 to 10.
  4. Slipping? Unblock, rescope, add help, or reset the date publicly.
  5. Close: verify against done, hand over to operations, credit people.

Use This When

You need a few engineers to get something done, like an automation, a runbook rewrite, a dashboard, or a fix that crosses a team. It isn't a full program. This playbook gives that work a light structure: a clear start, a steady middle, and a real finish. You stay the leader, not the project manager.

During

At every check-in, ask each owner PACE: Progress, Asks, Confidence, and End with owners.

  • Async status every 2–3 days, in the channel, using the 4-line template below. It takes two minutes to write and saves meetings.
  • One 10-minute midpoint checkpoint: are we on track for done-means and the date? If not, decide now.
  • What you watch for:
    • Confidence stuck at "10" right up to the deadline. That's watermelon status: green outside, red inside.
    • "Almost done" for more than two updates.
    • An owner who's gone quiet, often because they've been pulled into incidents.
    • A dependency nobody is chasing.
Status update (4 lines)
Done:       [what moved since last update]
Next:       [the next visible result, and when]
Blocked:    [what / who, or "nothing"]
Confidence: [1-10] for [target date]

When It's Slipping

  1. Unblock: "What are you waiting on?" If it's another team, that's yours to chase, manager to manager.
  2. Rescope: "What's the smallest version that still solves the problem?" Ship that, and park the rest.
  3. Add help or swap the owner. If the owner keeps getting pulled into incidents, protect their focus time, or move the work.
  4. Reset the date, openly. Set a new date with a checkpoint in the middle, and post it. Never let a date silently drift.
Reset message
Update on [task]: we're resetting the target from [old date] to [new date].
Why: [honest reason, e.g. "the owner was pulled into 3 incidents" / "scope was bigger than we thought"].
What we changed: [rescope / added help / unblocked X].
New checkpoint: [date]. Done still means: [done-means].

Closing

  • Verify against done-means. A demo, a graph, or a removed runbook step, not "it's merged."
  • Hand it over to operations. If anything changes for operations, update the runbooks, dashboards, and alerts, and walk the NOC lead through it.
  • Log leftovers as tracked items with an owner and a date.
  • Send the close-out note below to the channel and to your boss.
  • Run a 15-minute retro for anything that took more than 2 weeks.
  • Give credit by name in the SRE Daily wins block, and update the project board.

Example in Practice

Kickoff (15 min). Two engineers will automate the queue-worker restart that the NOC does by hand about six times a week. Done means "no manual restarts for 14 days, and the NOC runbook step removed." Ana owns the automation, by the 14th. Sam owns the service account from the platform team, by the 7th. Out of scope: fixing the underlying leak (the pod's job, tracked separately).

Day 6. Sam's update says "Blocked: platform team hasn't responded. Confidence 4." You message the platform manager the same day, and the account arrives on day 8. The target resets openly from the 14th to the 16th, with a new checkpoint.

Closing. Ana shows the graph: zero manual restarts for 14 days. The runbook step is deleted, and the NOC lead is walked through it. The close-out note goes to your boss, Ana and Sam get called out in the SRE Daily, and the 15-minute retro produces one change: request service accounts at kickoff, not mid-task.

What Actually Happens

Incidents eat the people doing the task.
Counter: name an interrupt handler, so the task owners are protected for the task's duration. Or reset the date openly. Don't pretend.
Updates stop after week one.
Counter: ask for the 4-line status in the SRE Daily engineering block until the habit sticks.
It's declared done without being done.
Counter: the done-means from the kickoff is the test. "Show me" is fair and expected.
Nobody tells the NOC, so the old runbook still says to restart it by hand.
Counter: the operations handover is a closing step, not a nice-to-have.
