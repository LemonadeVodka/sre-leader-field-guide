Monday 10:00: The 10-Minute Sweep

Marco: "On Track" for the Third Week

P10:01Progress

Marco: "Restart automation: on track."

C10:01Confidence

You: "Confidence in Friday, 1 to 10?"

Marco: "Uh… 5."

You: "What would make it a 9?"

Marco: "Platform has to approve a service account so the script can restart the worker. I raised a ticket two weeks ago."

A10:02Asks

You: "Thanks. That's mine to chase. I'll talk to Jordan today. Next time something's waiting more than a week, tell me in the sweep, even if it feels small."

The nuance: Marco wasn't hiding anything. "On track" meant "my part is done." The confidence question is what turned a watermelon (green outside, red inside) into a blocker you can actually clear.

Lin: "Not Much Progress"

P10:03Progress

Lin: "NOC runbook update: not much progress, sorry."

A10:03Asks

You: "No need to apologize. What's been taking your time?"

Lin: "Since Tuesday the NOC's been escalating everything to me directly, every night. I've been handling maybe ten a night."

The nuance: Lin's hidden work is legitimately more urgent than her planned work. It's also exactly the problem her runbook action would fix. The answer isn't "work harder," it's to make the hidden work visible and trade something off.

E10:04End with owners

You: "Let's put the night escalations on the board so they count. Marco takes them Tuesday and Thursday this week. Lin, runbook draft by Friday with Asha. I'll check with you Wednesday. And let's talk for 10 minutes after this."

Tomás: "It's Going to Be Late"

C10:05Confidence

Tomás: "Dashboard migration: honestly, a 4. It's going to be late. The old dashboards use queries nobody understands."

You: "Thank you for saying it now, not on the due date. That's exactly what I want. Question: if we stopped this for a month, what would break?"

Tomás: "…Nothing, really. It's tidy-up."

E10:06End with owners

You: "Then let's pause it. Tomás, can you help Kai finish the alert routing instead? I'll tell Priya the migration moves to next month, and why."

The nuance: the right answer to a late item is sometimes to stop it, not to push harder. Say out loud what you're letting slip, so nobody thinks it was forgotten.

Kai: "Merged"

P10:07Progress

Kai: "Alert routing: done. Merged on Friday."

You: "Great. The done-means from the postmortem was 'a test alert reaches the NOC and they acknowledge it in a drill.' Has that happened?"

Kai: "Not yet. Asha's team is on a different shift."

E10:08End with owners

You: "Then it's nearly done, not done. Kai and Tomás run the drill with Asha's shift on Wednesday at their 09:00. Good work getting it merged."

The Owners Who Aren't Yours

Actions #3 (Dee, platform) and #4 (Sam, Assistant pod) belong to people you don't manage. You ask the same four questions, lighter, by direct message, and never in front of their team.

Message to Dee (platform)
Hi Dee, quick check on the quota alert from Thursday's postmortem. How's it going, and is anything in the way?
No pressure on the date; I just want to report it accurately to Priya on Friday.

Dee replies: "Alert's half done. But heads up: Jordan says the pre-approved failover isn't happening this quarter. We're flat out on the launch."

Sam doesn't reply at all. You try once more on Wednesday, then ask him in person at the pod leads' sync.

The nuance: the most important fix from the postmortem has just stalled, and it's not your call. That's Wednesday's problem: Nobody will fix it.

What I'd Do Differently

Honest Notes

  • Priya would have preferred green. Amber with a plan is more useful to her than green that turns red in two weeks, even if she doesn't say so on Friday.
  • I let Marco's blocker sit for two weeks because I wasn't asking about confidence until now.
  • Lin's night escalations were a symptom of the same NOC runbook gap from Tuesday. The sweep surfaced it; the postmortem action fixes it.
  • Chasing Sam three times costs goodwill. If his action stays silent, it goes through his manager, not a fourth message from me.
