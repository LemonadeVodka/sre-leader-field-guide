Tuesday 21:40: The Assistant Is Timing Out

A realistic incident, minute by minute: running LEAD when your central SRE team can see the problem but can't change anything.

Who's Who (fictional)

You
Senior manager, central SRE. Your team can see problems but can't change production.
Ravi
NOC analyst on the offshore night shift. Asha is his shift lead.
Marco
Your SRE on call. Good at the technical side, quieter about taking charge.
Sam, Noor
Assistant pod: Sam is the lead, Noor is the engineer who made last week's config change.
Dee, Jordan
Platform pod: Dee is on call, Jordan is the pod manager.
Priya
Your VP.

What Your Team Can and Can't Do

What a central SRE team with no controls can and can't do
Your team canYour team can't (yet)
See it: Azure Monitor, App Insights, GrafanaChange production or roll anything back
Declare and page in PagerDutyApprove or block a pod's deploys and config changes
Coordinate the bridge and own the commsDecide another pod's fix, or its priorities
Escalate to managers and execsMake the model provider move faster
Write things down: timeline, decisions, risksSpend budget or buy capacity

So every move below works through influence, not control: making things visible, asking at the right moment, escalating on a clock, and writing decisions down.

21:40: The First LEAD Pass

You're at home. PagerDuty fires a Sev 2 that the NOC opened at 21:31: "assistant-gateway 5xx above threshold." You join the bridge. Four people are on it, all looking at the same Grafana dashboard. Nobody is talking.

L21:41Leader

You: "Hi all, I've just joined. Who's leading this right now?"

Marco: "I guess me? I've been looking at the gateway."

You: "Great. Marco's incident commander. I'll take comms and escalations so Marco can stay on the problem. Ravi, please keep the incident channel updated with anything you see."

Plate check: you took comms and escalations, not the fix. The nuance: Marco was already doing the work well. Taking over as IC would have slowed him down and told the room you don't trust him. Naming him, and taking the parts he's worst placed to do, is the stronger move.

E21:42Effect

You: "Ravi, in plain words, what are users seeing?"

Ravi: "The alert says 5xx above threshold on assistant-gateway."

You: "Thanks. Marco, can you turn that into users: how many, since when, and is it getting worse?"

Marco: "About 30% of requests timing out, mostly EU. Started around 21:25. It's climbing."

What Ravi got right and wrong: right, he declared in PagerDuty within 6 minutes of noticing. Wrong, he read back the alert instead of looking at the impact, and his note had no start time. That's coaching for later, not now.

A21:44Action

You: "What have we ruled out, and what's our best guess?"

Marco: "Not CPU, not the database. My guess is the model provider."

You: "Is anyone checking the provider? And did anything change in the last day?"

Marco (a minute later): "There's a Grafana annotation. The Assistant pod changed the retry config at 21:10, retries from 2 to 5."

Not yours to decide: you can't roll back that change, and neither can Marco. The Assistant pod owns it, and nobody from that pod is on the bridge. The lever you have: get them here.

D21:46Deadline

You: "Who are we waiting on?"

Ravi: "I posted in the platform channel at 21:33."

You: "Thanks, Ravi. A channel post isn't a page, so let's page them properly. I'm paging platform and the Assistant pod now in PagerDuty. If nobody acknowledges by 21:56, I'll call their managers. Next exec update at 22:00."

The nuance: 13 minutes passed between Ravi's Slack post and a real page. The NOC runbook didn't list the platform pager. That's a system gap, and it goes into the postmortem.

21:56: Second Pass, and the Deadline Hits

D21:56Deadline

Sam from the Assistant pod has joined. Platform hasn't acknowledged. You check their escalation policy in PagerDuty: it has one person on it and no secondary.

You (calling Jordan, platform manager): "Jordan, sorry to call late. We have a Sev 2, about a third of EU requests failing since 21:25. Dee was paged at 21:46 and hasn't acknowledged. Can you reach her, or anyone else on platform, in the next 10 minutes?"

Jordan: "Ugh. Yes, I'll call her."

E21:58Effect

Marco: "It's 34% now in EU."

A21:59Action

Marco: "I'd roll back the retry change. Five retries against a struggling provider makes it worse."

Sam: "That change fixed a bug for enterprise customers last week. If we roll back, they get errors again."

You: "That's a real trade-off, and it's your call, Sam. Can we compare: a third of EU users failing right now, against that bug for a handful of enterprise users for an hour or two?"

Sam (after a pause): "OK. Rolling back to 2 retries. I'll do it."

You (in the channel): "Decision 22:02: Sam rolls back retries to 2. Owner Sam. We check the error rate at 22:12."

Not yours to decide: the rollback. The lever you have: putting both risks side by side, out loud, so the owner can decide quickly. Then you write the decision down with a name and a time.

22:12: Third Pass, Partial Progress

E22:12Effect

Marco: "Down from 34% to 18%. Better, not fixed."

A22:14Action

Dee from platform joined at 22:08. Her phone had been on silent.

Dee: "The provider is throttling our EU deployment. We're getting 429s, so we've hit a quota. I can fail EU traffic over to the US region. Latency goes up by maybe 400 milliseconds. But failover isn't pre-approved, so I need Jordan's OK."

You: "What's the other option?"

Dee: "Ask the provider to raise the quota. That's a support ticket. Could be hours."

Not yours to decide, twice: the provider's quota isn't in anyone's control tonight, and failover needs a manager's approval because nobody agreed it in advance. The lever you have: getting Jordan to the decision fast, with both options laid out.

D22:16Deadline

You: "Dee, please open the provider ticket now, in parallel. I'll get Jordan on the bridge for the failover decision. Can we have that decision by 22:30?"

22:30: Exec Heat

D22:31Deadline

Priya joins the bridge.

Priya: "Marco, when will this be fixed? I need to update the CEO."

You: "Priya, I've got the updates. Can I give you the full picture in a side thread, so Marco can keep going?"

Priya (in the thread): "I need an ETA."

You: "I can't give you a reliable one, and I don't want to give you a wrong one. Here's what I can say: we have a failover decision by 22:40. If we fail over, errors should drop within 10 minutes. Next update 22:45, sooner if it changes."

Priya: "That's not great."

The nuance: she's unhappy, and that's OK. An honest "I don't know, here's when I will" costs you a little tonight. A made-up ETA that slips costs you a lot more next time.

22:40: Mitigation, and the Messy Middle

A22:36Action

Jordan approves the failover at 22:36. Dee starts it at 22:41. By 22:49, errors are under 1%. EU responses are about 350 ms slower, so the first word appears later.

You: "Is this resolved, or mitigated? What would make us call it resolved?"

Marco: "Mitigated. Resolved is when we're back in EU with the quota raised and errors normal."

You: "Agreed. We stay on the backup region overnight. Ravi, can you watch the error rate and post it every 15 minutes until 23:30? If it goes over 2%, page Marco."

Why give the NOC a job: Ravi has been sitting quietly for an hour. A clear, small task keeps him involved, and it's how the NOC learns what "watching" an incident looks like.

23:10: Closing It Out

D23:10Deadline

You: "Before we drop: failback tomorrow, owner Dee. The provider quota ticket, owner Jordan. Sam, can your pod own the RCA draft, since the retry change is part of it? Postmortem Thursday at 14:00. Thank you, everyone. Marco, great job tonight."

Sam: "Fine. Though honestly this was the provider."

The nuance: Sam is already defending his pod. Don't argue at 23:10. That's what Thursday's postmortem is for, with evidence and ground rules.

What I'd Do Differently

Honest Notes

  • I asked the Deadline question 5 minutes too late. "Who are we waiting on?" belongs in the first 2 minutes, before anyone gets comfortable.
  • I didn't know platform had one person on its escalation policy. That's a risk, and it's now on my list for Thursday.
  • Nobody could pull the failover without a manager's OK. We lost about 20 minutes to that. A pre-approved lever would have saved it, but my team can't create one; it's platform's decision. That thread runs all week, and it ends in the risk register.
  • Ravi needs coaching, not blame. He declared quickly. He needs the platform pager in his runbook and a habit of including the start time.
