Reliability Field Guide

Running Projects in Linear Without a PM

A 15-minute weekly sweep, the hygiene to ask for, a check-in that walks the board, and a five-line project update your VP will read.

Quick Card: Your Weekly 15 Minutes in Linear

  1. Quiet: In Progress with no update in 5+ days. Ask the owner for the next step and date.
  2. Blocked: every one has a "blocked by" link, a who, and a follow-up date.
  3. Unowned: issues in active projects with no assignee. Leads assign or close.
  4. Undated: active projects with no target date. Ask for a date and a confidence number.
  5. Update: post a 5-line project update for your VP.

Use This When

You lost your project manager and you're holding the work together in Linear. You don't need to become a PM. You need a short, predictable routine that finds stuck work early, keeps owners clear, and gives your leadership an honest picture. This page is that routine.

The Weekly Sweep (15 Minutes)

Save these as Linear views once (filters on status, assignee, due date, and last updated), then run them every Monday before your team check-in.

Linear Hygiene to Ask For

You don't enforce these by doing them yourself. Ask your leads to make them the team's habit, and check in the sweep.

  • One owner per issue. "The team" isn't an owner.
  • Blocked issues have a "blocked by" link to the issue they're waiting on, and a comment with who, since when, and the follow-up date. See the blocked comment template.
  • Every active project has a target date and a weekly project update.
  • Done means done: verified and handed to operations (runbook updated, NOC told), not just merged.
  • Interrupts get an issue too, even small ones, so hidden work is visible.

The Cycle Check-In: Walk the Board, Not the People

Run your team check-in from the Linear board, right to left: closest to done first. It keeps the focus on finishing work instead of a round of status reports. For each item, PACE:

The Weekly Project Update

Linear projects can carry a status update with a health (on track, at risk, off track). Keep yours to five lines, so your VP can read it in 20 seconds.

Project update (5 lines)
Health: [on track / at risk / off track]
Moved: [what finished this week]
Next: [what finishes next week]
Blocked: [what, on whom, since when, escalated to whom]
Date: [target date], confidence [n]/10

End of Cycle: 10-Minute Look Back

When a cycle ends, look at what rolled over and group it. One change per cycle is plenty.

Questions to Ask

What Actually Happens

  • The board drifts from reality within two weeks unless someone looks every week. That someone is you, for 15 minutes.
  • Engineers hate updating tickets. Ask for one comment when status changes, not daily updates.
  • You'll be tempted to update issues yourself. Don't; ask the owner. Otherwise you become the team's admin.
  • Leadership reads the project update, not the board. Make the five lines honest.
