SRE Leader's Field Guide

Where Are We?

Find out what people are working on and where things really stand, without micromanaging and without sounding out of touch.

Quick Card: PACE, for Each Owner

  1. P Progress: "What moved since last time?"
  2. A Asks: "What's blocking you, or who are you waiting on? Is anyone waiting on you?"
  3. C Confidence: "How confident are you in the date, 1 to 10?" Under 7: "What would make it a 9?"
  4. E End with owners: "So: [owner] does [next step] by [date]. I'll check on [date]."

And say it out loud: "Telling me early that it's late is always safe."

Use This When

You need to know what people are working on and where things stand, often across engineers who don't report to a project manager. You're the de facto PM. This page keeps it light: ask about change and blockers, not activity, and make bad news safe to deliver early.

The Weekly Sweep

One short, predictable check-in a week beats constant pinging. Same day, same format, same channel.

Late is fine. Late and quiet isn't.

Decode the Answer

Status words hide a lot. When you hear one of these, ask the follow-up. It's not distrust; it's how you find problems while they're still small.

They sayIt often meansAsk next
"Almost done"The easy 80% is done. The hard 20% isn't started."What's actually left, and what's the riskiest part?"
"On track" (every week)Nobody has checked against the date, or it's green outside and red inside."What's the part you're least sure about?"
"Should be fine"Untested."How will we know it's fine? When do we test it?"
"Waiting on them"They sent one message and stopped."Who exactly, since when, and do you want me to escalate?"
"Working on it"Activity with no next milestone."What's the next thing that will be done, and when?"
"It's complicated"Scope grew, or nobody has broken it down."What's the smallest version that still solves the problem?"
"I've been busy with other stuff"Hidden work is eating their time."What's the other stuff? Let's put it on the board."

Keep a One-Page Tracker

You don't need a project tool. One table, updated after the sweep, is enough to answer your boss in 30 seconds.

Status tracker
Item              Owner    Target   Status     Confidence  Blocked on        Next check
[task / project]  [name]   [date]   on track   8/10        -                 [date]
[task / project]  [name]   [date]   at risk    5/10        [team / person]   [date]
[task / project]  [name]   [date]   late       3/10        [decision]        [date]

When something is slipping, use the four moves in Running a Task: unblock, rescope, add help, or reset the date publicly.

Example in Practice

The Automation That Was "On Track" for Three Weeks

A runbook automation has been "on track" in three sweeps. This week you ask for date confidence: "about a 5." You ask what would make it a 9: "Platform has to approve the service account, and we've been waiting two weeks." You ask who exactly: "I sent one ticket." You message the platform manager that afternoon, the account is approved two days later, and you reset the date publicly by one week.

The engineer wasn't hiding anything. Nobody had asked a question that made the blocker visible.

What Actually Happens

  • People hear "status" as "are you working hard enough." Ask about blockers and change, and act on blockers fast. They'll start telling you sooner.
  • Everyone says "on track" until the week it's due. The confidence number is what breaks that habit.
  • The sweep grows into a 45-minute meeting. Hold it to one line each and take details offline.
  • You'll be tempted to ask every day. Don't. Predictable weekly beats anxious daily, and it builds trust.
