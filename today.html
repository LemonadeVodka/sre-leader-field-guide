Reliability Field Guide

Questions for Today

Exact words for the moments in your week: the incident bridge, stuck incidents, the NOC, postmortems, status check-ins, the perf meeting, peers and your VP, and when you don't know. Each with the follow-up for the weak answer.

One Rule: Own Decisions, Not Work

You own decisions, escalations, exec comms, and naming owners. Investigation, fixes, chasing, and write-ups get a named owner, usually not you. Before you say "I'll…", ask yourself: is this only something I can do? If not, name an owner. Section 9 has the words.

How to Use This Page

Find the moment you're in. Say the question exactly as written. If you get the weak answer, use the follow-up. Bring three, not eight. Each section has a Copy button so you can paste the list into your notes before you walk in.

1. On an Incident Bridge

The moment you join, in this order. Then repeat the last four every 15 minutes. (This is LEAD.)

  1. "Who's leading this right now?"

    If they say "We're all looking at it" → say "[Name], can you lead until the owning team joins? I'll handle exec updates."

  2. "What are users seeing, in one sentence?"

    If they say the alert name → ask "In plain words: what can't users do, and roughly how many?"

  3. "When did it start, and how do we know?"

    If they say "A while ago" → ask "Can someone check the first error in App Insights or Grafana and post the time?"

  4. "Is it getting worse, better, or flat in the last 15 minutes?"

    If they say "Hard to say" → ask "Can someone post the error-rate graph in the channel every 15 minutes?"

  5. "Which part is slow: the edge, our prep, the model, or the stream back?"

    If they say "It's just slow" → ask "Can we see those four pieces separately? (where the seconds go)"

  6. "What changed in the last 24 hours?"

    If they say "Nothing" → ask "Did anyone check Grafana annotations, deploys, config, and the model provider's status page?"

  7. "What have we ruled out, and how?"

    If they say "We've looked at everything" → say "Let's write the ruled-out list in the channel so we stop re-checking."

  8. "What's the safest thing we can do in the next 15 minutes?"

    If they say "We're still investigating" → ask "Is there a rollback, failover, or flag we could use while we investigate?"

  9. "Who are we waiting on, and did they acknowledge in PagerDuty?"

    If they say "We pinged them in Slack" → say "[Name], page them properly now. If no acknowledgement in 10 minutes, I'll call their manager."

  10. "When's the next update, and who's sending it?"

    If they say nothing → say "I'll send updates every 15 minutes. Next one at [time]. Team, keep working."

2. When It's Been Stuck for 30+ Minutes

No progress, no theory, or nobody who can change it.

  1. "Let's pause for 60 seconds. What do we know, what don't we know, what's next?"

    If they say nobody can summarize → say "[Name], can you post it in the channel? Everyone else, correct anything wrong."

  2. "If you had to bet: our code, our infrastructure, or something we depend on?"

    If they say "Could be anything" → say "It's a bet, not a promise. What's the quickest way to check it?"

  3. "If we only had 10 minutes before this got much worse, what would we try?"

    If they say "Nothing's safe" → ask "What's worse: trying it and being wrong, or waiting another 30 minutes?"

  4. "Who else knows this system, even if they're not on call?"

    If they say "Only one person, and they're off" → ask "Who's their manager? I'll make that one call. It's also going in the postmortem."

  5. "Who has the access to change this, and are they on the bridge?"

    If they say "Not us" → ask "Which team, and what do they need from us so they can start immediately?"

  6. "Are we still fixing it, or should we be working around it?"

    If they say "We're close" → ask "What result would prove it, and by what time? If not by then, we work around it."

  7. "If there's no fix in an hour, whose decision is it what we do?"

    If they say "Not sure" → ask "Who has the authority? Let's get them on the thread now, so the decision is ready."

3. Talking to the NOC

Kind, specific, and firm. They're your eyes; help them be good ones.

  1. "In your own words, what have you seen? Not what the alert says."

    If they read the alert back → ask "Can you check one thing: does it fail for everyone, or only some users?"

  2. "Which runbook step are you on, and what happened when you did it?"

    If they say "We followed the runbook" → ask "Which step exactly? Please post what you did in the channel."

  3. "Who did you escalate to, and what exactly did you send them?"

    If they say "We told SRE" → say "Let's resend it with impact, start time, what you tried, and a link."

  4. "What do you need from me to take the next step?"

    If they say "Nothing" while nothing moves → say "Then here's your next step: page [team] now and paste this summary."

  5. "What would have made you escalate sooner?"

    If they say "Nothing" → ask "Was there a moment you weren't sure? What stopped you asking?"

  6. (At handoff) "What's the one thing the next shift must not miss?"

    If they say "All quiet" → ask "Anything you saw but didn't escalate? Even small?"

  7. (To the shift lead) "Where did the team get stuck this week, and what would help?"

    If they say "Everything's fine" → say "Let's look at last Tuesday's escalation together. I'd like to understand it."

4. Running a Postmortem

Before, during, and after. (This is PREP → SAFER → CLOSE.)

  1. (To the author, the day before) "Does the draft have impact numbers, a tool timeline, and more than one factor?"

    If it says "Root cause: X. Factors: none" → ask "Can we add [the change / the gap]? Not to blame; it's the part we can fix."

  2. (Opening) "Blameless, facts first, and we leave with owned actions. OK with everyone?"

    If someone smirks → say "If anyone hears blame, including from me, call it out."

  3. "Who was affected, for how long, and how did we find out: us or customers?"

    If they dispute the numbers → ask "What do the dashboards say? Let's agree the number before we go on."

  4. "What did you know at that point, and what made sense to do?"

    If they go quiet → say "[Name], you were on shift. What did you see first?"

  5. "What made that easy to get wrong?"

    If they say "Human error" → ask "What check would have caught it? If none exists, that's our action."

  6. "Why didn't we catch it sooner?"

    If they say "The NOC should have" → ask "Partly true. Where did the alert go, and could they see it?"

  7. "If we only did one thing, which?"

    If they list ten → say "Let's rank them. Top four get owners today; the rest we park or drop."

  8. "Who owns it, by when, and what does done mean?"

    If they say "The team" → ask "Which one person? If nobody can say yes today, it goes on the risk register."

  9. (30 days later) "Is it done with evidence, and has it happened again?"

    If they say "Merged" → ask "Has it been tested for real, like a drill or a test alert?"

5. Status Check-Ins and 1:1s

Ask every owner the same way, every week. (This is PACE.)

  1. "What moved since last time?"

    If they say "Working on it" → ask "What's the next thing that will be done, and when?"

  2. "What's blocking you, or who are you waiting on?"

    If they say "Waiting on them" → ask "Who exactly, since when, and what have you tried? If it's stuck by Thursday, tell me and I'll escalate."

  3. "How confident are you in the date, 1 to 10?"

    If they say under 7 → ask "What would make it a 9?"

  4. "What are you doing that isn't on the board?"

    If they say "Nothing" → ask "Any interrupts, favors, or NOC escalations eating your week?"

  5. "Is anyone waiting on you?"

    If they say "Not that I know of" → say "Let's check with [team] so they know when to expect it."

  6. "It's been 'almost done' for a while. What's actually left?"

    If they list the hard part → ask "What's the smallest version that still solves the problem?"

  7. "If this slips, when will we know, and how will you tell me?"

    If they say "It won't slip" → say "Telling me early that it's late is always safe."

  8. (Closing) "So: you'll do [next step] by [date], and I'll check on [date]. Right?"

    If they hesitate → ask "What date would you actually commit to?"

6. Performance Engineering Meeting

You don't need to read a flame graph. You need these.

  1. "What's the one number users would feel this week, and is it better or worse?"

    If they show CPU graphs → ask "Which graph would prove a customer right if they said it's slow?"

  2. "What's p95 time to first token this week versus last, and against target?"

    If they say "We don't track that" → ask "Can we start? It's what users feel first in the assistant."

  3. "What got slower, and what changed right before it?"

    If they say "Not sure" → ask "Can we check deploys and Grafana annotations from that day?"

  4. "Is it our code, more load, or the model provider?"

    If they say "The provider" → ask "What would we see if it were ours? Let's check that first."

  5. "How much slower can this get before users complain?"

    If they say "Faster is better" → say "Let's agree a target and put it on the scorecard."

  6. "What's coming in 30 days that changes load or cost?"

    If they say "Nothing planned" → ask "Any launches, campaigns, or model changes from product?"

  7. "If we fix only one thing before next week, which, and who owns it?"

    If they list seven → say "Pick one. We'll check next week whether it moved the number."

7. With Peers, Pods, and Your VP

You have no authority over most of these people. These questions work anyway.

  1. "Help me understand what's in the way."

    If they say "It's not a priority" → ask "What would have to be true for it to make the list?"

  2. "What's the smallest version of this, and who on your side would own it?"

    If they say "Nobody has time" → ask "Then whose decision is it to make room for it?"

  3. "Whose decision is this really: yours, mine, or someone above us?"

    If they say "Not sure" → say "Let's take it to them together with a short write-up."

  4. (To the decider) "Fix it, fund it, or accept it until a date? Any is fine."

    If they say "Yeah fine" → say "To confirm: accepted until [date], with these mitigations. Shout if wrong."

  5. "What do you need from SRE that you're not getting?"

    If they say "Nothing" → ask "When did we last slow you down?"

  6. (To your VP) "Here's what I know, what I don't, and when I'll know more. What else do you need?"

    If they say "I need an ETA" → say "I can't give a reliable one yet. Next update at [time]."

  7. (To your VP) "Are you OK with amber and a plan, rather than green that turns red?"

    If they push for green → say "Here's what would make it green, and what it costs."

8. When You Don't Know the Answer

Lines to say, not questions to avoid. Saying them well builds credibility.

  1. "I don't know yet. [Name] will find out; you'll have it by [time]."

    If they push → say "I'd rather give you the right answer at 3 than a wrong one now."

  2. "Walk me through it like I'm new to this service."

    If they use jargon → ask "What does that mean for users?"

  3. "What would you do if it were your call?"

    If they say "It's not my call" → say "Understood. I'd still like your view before I decide."

  4. "I'm not the right person to judge the fix. Who is, and what do you need from me?"

    If they say "Just you" → ask "Then what are the options and the risk of each, in plain words?"

  5. "What would we expect to see if that's true?"

    If they say "It's obvious" → say "Humor me. Show me the graph."

  6. "What's the risk if we're wrong?"

    If they say "There isn't one" → ask "Then how do we undo it if there is?"

9. Keep It With the Owner

For the moment someone hands you work that isn't yours. You own the decision; someone else owns the work.

  1. "Who owns this today?"

    If they say "You?" → say "I'll own the decision. Who owns the work?"

  2. "What would you do if I weren't here?"

    If they say "Wait for you" → say "Do what you'd do, and tell me if it goes wrong."

  3. "What do you need from me: a decision, an introduction, or air cover?"

    If they say "Just help" → ask "Which one of those three?"

  4. "Can you bring me a recommendation, not just the problem?"

    If they say "I don't know what to do" → say "Bring two options and your pick by [time]."

  5. "Is this mine to fix, or mine to escalate?"

    If they say "Yours to fix" → ask "Then who on my team owns it, with me as backup?"

  6. "Who on my team could own this, with me as backup?"

    If they say "Only you know it" → say "Then that's a gap. Who could learn it this month?"

  7. "What happens if this waits until next week?"

    If they say "Nothing much" → say "Then it goes on the list, not my desk today."

  8. "Whose name goes on this before we leave?"

    If they say "The team" → say "One name, please. I'll check in on [date]."
