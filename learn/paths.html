Reliability Field Guide

Learning Paths

The Incident Leader Path (8 weeks) and the De Facto PM Path (3 weeks). About 15 minutes a day, built around practice in real incidents and real meetings.

How the Paths Work

About 15 minutes a day. Each week has one goal, a short reading list from this site, one behavior to practice on a real bridge or in a real meeting, and the daily lessons that go with it. Practice beats reading: the week only counts if you did the practice.

Set the day you started, and the home page will show which week you're on. (Saved only in this browser.)

The Incident Leader Path (8 Weeks)

Week 1 Command Basics

Goal: on every bridge you join, a leader is named in the first two minutes, and it's usually not you.

Read: LEAD · Questions for the bridge · Tuesday's incident

Practice this week: say the IC line out loud on your next incident: "[Name], can you lead until the owning team joins? I'll handle exec updates."

Lessons: Name the IC out loud · How long until someone led? · The 15-minute rhythm

You'll know it's working when: engineers start naming an IC before you ask.

Week 2 Impact and Severity

Goal: the impact is stated in one sentence by minute 10, and severity is never a debate.

Read: Observability Day One · Impact is unclear · Incident Program

Practice this week: on every incident, ask "what are users seeing, how many, since when, and is it getting worse?" before anything technical.

Lessons: Impact in one sentence · Are the severity rules written down? · Declare early

You'll know it's working when: updates lead with user impact without you prompting.

Week 3 When It's Stuck

Goal: you recognize the stuck pattern within a few minutes and use the matching question.

Read: Move a Stuck Incident · Stuck Incident: Deep Dive

Practice this week: agree an escalation clock (15 minutes to secondary, 30 to manager) with one pod lead.

Lessons: What have we ruled out? · Escalate on a clock · Know your levers

You'll know it's working when: no wait on another team goes past 15 minutes without a name and a backup.

Week 4 Communication Under Pressure

Goal: execs get calm, timed updates, and engineers never get pulled off the fix to answer them.

Read: Exec heat · Incident bridge ground rules · Escalation and exec comms

Practice this week: send an exec update every 15–30 minutes on the next incident, using your pinned template.

Lessons: Pin your exec update template · The honest 'no ETA' · Who tells customers?

You'll know it's working when: your VP stops joining the bridge because your updates arrive first.

Week 5 The NOC and Handoffs

Goal: the NOC declares early, escalates with a good packet, and hands over cleanly across time zones.

Read: With the offshore NOC · The NOC is lost · Long-running incidents

Practice this week: in your NOC sync, have one analyst describe an incident in their own words; coach one thing.

Lessons: The NOC's own words · Find the missing pager · Handoffs across time zones

You'll know it's working when: NOC escalations include impact, start time, and what was tried, every time.

Week 6 AI-Specific Incidents

Goal: when the assistant is slow or failing, you can point to the hop and ask the right owner.

Read: Request Map · Latency: TTFT and TTLT · Token limits · Model providers

Practice this week: spend 15 minutes with an engineer filling in one box of your private map.

Lessons: Which 429? · Which TTFT pieces can you see? · How close to the token limit? · Is Cosmos DB near its limit?

You'll know it's working when: "the assistant is slow" turns into "the model's first word got slower" or "our prep grew" within 10 minutes.

Week 7 Learning From Incidents

Goal: every Sev 1–2 gets a postmortem within 5 days, and its actions land.

Read: Owning Postmortems · Thursday's postmortem · Judging an RCA

Practice this week: do the 5-minute leader debrief after every incident you're part of.

Lessons: Book the next postmortem · Do actions have owners? · Your leader debrief · Catch blame and turn it

You'll know it's working when: your debrief notes show the same mistake less often.

Week 8 Building the Program

Goal: incidents get better because the system around them gets better: alerts, on-call, drills, and decisions about risk.

Read: Incident Program · Alerting and Paging · Risk Register

Practice this week: run a 10-minute tabletop with one SRE: "the model provider is throttling EU."

Lessons: Run a tabletop · Kill one noisy alert · How was on-call? · Don't let MTTR fool you

You'll know it's working when: you're coaching others to lead bridges, not leading every one yourself.

The De Facto PM Path (3 Weeks)

Week 1 PACE and the Weekly Sweep

Goal: you know where every active project stands, every week, in 15 minutes.

Read: Running projects in Linear · Where are we? · Monday's sweep

Practice this week: run the 15-minute Linear sweep on Monday, then walk the board in your check-in.

Lessons: Find the quiet issues · Ask for the confidence number · Walk the board

You'll know it's working when: nothing surprises you on its due date.

Week 2 "Waiting On" and Linear Hygiene

Goal: every wait has a name, a date, and a next step, and you sound confident asking about it.

Read: When they say "I'm waiting on…"

Practice this week: on every "waiting on" you hear, ask "who exactly, since when, what did you ask for?" and "what can you move today?"

Lessons: Who exactly, since when? · Blocked-by link and a date · What can you move today? · Split it so part ships

You'll know it's working when: engineers bring the who, when, and next step before you ask.

Week 3 Updates Upward, Risks, and Saying No

Goal: your VP gets an honest five-line update weekly, and stuck cross-team work becomes a decision, not endless chasing.

Read: The weekly project update · Risk register · Saying "it's late" upward

Practice this week: post one honest amber update with a plan.

Lessons: Write the update in 5 lines · Say "this is late" · Stop chasing, log the risk · Say no, kindly

You'll know it's working when: your VP asks you fewer status questions because the update already answered them.
