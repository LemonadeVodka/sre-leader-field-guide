Toil Reduction & Automation

Measure toil, rank it by real cost, automate it or hand it back to the pod, and take manual work off the NOC.

Why It Matters

  • For the business: toil (manual, repetitive work that grows with traffic) quietly eats the team's capacity. Every hour of toil is an hour not spent preventing the next incident.
  • For you: measured toil is your strongest headcount and priority argument. "40% of SRE time goes to manual restarts caused by [pod]'s service" gets action that "we're busy" never will.

What the Workbook Says

  • Toil is manual, repetitive, automatable, reactive, without lasting value, and grows with service size. Not all operational work is toil. (Workbook, Ch. 6: Eliminating Toil (opens in a new tab))
  • Measure before you fix. Identify and quantify toil so you prioritize by real cost, not by what's most annoying.
  • Treat toil reduction as engineering work, with its own time, priority, and success measures.
  • Start small and safe: automate well-understood steps first, and keep humans in the loop for risky actions.
  • The SRE norm is to keep toil to a minority of the team's time, commonly around half at most. (SRE Book, Ch. 5 (opens in a new tab))

Reality Check

  • Much of your toil is caused by pod code (restarts, cleanups, workarounds). The best "automation" is often handing it back with data: "fix the cause."
  • The offshore NOC does toil too. Lookups and copy-paste steps in runbooks are the easiest wins, and they reduce errors.
  • Firefighting crowds out automation. Without protected time, it never happens.
  • Some automation needs pod access or service accounts, which take time. Request them early.

Before You Start

  • The team agrees to tag their time for two weeks (rough is fine).
  • A protected slice of time, such as one engineer at 50%, for the top item.
  • The alerting cleanup done or underway. Noisy pages are often the biggest toil.

How to Sell It

To your boss
Two weeks of time tagging shows SRE spends about [X%] on toil, about [N] engineer-hours a month. The top 3 items are [list].
Plan: automate [item 1] ourselves, get [pod] to fix the cause of [item 2], and take [item 3] off the NOC.
Target: toil under [50%] by [quarter end], with the hours reinvested in [SLOs / alerting]. Ask: protect one engineer at 50% for 6 weeks.
To a pod manager
Your service generates about [N] hours a month of manual work for SRE and the NOC: [task] about [count] times.
We can keep absorbing it, but the real fix is [cause]. Can we get it into your next planning cycle?
We'll share the before-and-after numbers so your team gets credit for the improvement.

How You'll Know It's Working

  • Leading: toil measured; the top 5 decided; one automation in progress at all times.
  • Lagging: toil % falling quarter over quarter; hours reinvested into reliability projects; fewer NOC errors on automated tasks.

What Actually Happens

Engineers automate what's fun, not what's costly.
Counter: rank strictly by hours per month. Fun projects wait.
Time tagging gets abandoned after three days.
Counter: keep it rough (three categories, daily), and explain what the data will win: headcount or pod fixes.
Automation creates a new failure mode.
Counter: start with safe, reversible actions. Add logging, and a human approval step for anything risky.
