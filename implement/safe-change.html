Safe Change: Canary, Rollback & Prompt/Model Changes

Make every change visible, gradual, and reversible, including prompts, model versions, and safety settings, without adding a CAB.

Why It Matters

  • For the business: most incidents start with a change. Making change small, gradual, visible, and reversible prevents incidents and shortens the ones that still happen.
  • For you: for an AI product, prompt and model changes are code changes. If they skip the safety steps, you'll keep getting "up but wrong" incidents nobody saw coming. This is also the modern answer to the CAB, and leading it shows you've moved from IT Ops to SRE thinking.

What the Workbook Says

  • Canarying means releasing a change to a small part of production first and comparing it against the rest before going wider. (Workbook, Ch. 16: Canarying Releases (opens in a new tab))
  • Choose canary metrics that reflect user impact, and give the canary enough time and traffic to tell the difference.
  • Configuration changes are a major source of outages, and deserve the same rigor as code: review, versioning, and gradual rollout. (Ch. 14: Configuration Design (opens in a new tab) · Ch. 15: Configuration Specifics (opens in a new tab))
  • Releases should be repeatable and automated, with rollback as a normal, practiced operation. (SRE Book, Ch. 8: Release Engineering (opens in a new tab))

Reality Check

  • Pods own the pipelines. SRE's job is to set the standard, make changes visible, and measure change-caused incidents, not to build every pipeline.
  • Prompts, model versions, and safety settings may be edited in a portal, outside any pipeline. Find these first, because they're your biggest blind spot.
  • Your IT Ops instinct may be to add approvals. Resist it: approvals slow change without catching much. Canaries and rollback catch the problems.
  • Feature flags and canaries need tooling (confirm what your pods use, for example Azure App Configuration).

Before You Start

  • Change-caused incidents counted from recent RCAs. This is your business case.
  • Pod managers willing to adopt a change standard.
  • A Grafana annotation source available (pipeline webhook or Activity Log query).

How to Sell It

To your boss
[X] of our last [Y] incidents started with a change, including [N] prompt or model changes that skipped any testing.
Instead of adding approvals (slow, and they don't catch much), I want every change type visible on our dashboards,
and the riskiest ones (prompts and models) canaried with fast rollback. Ask: back a change standard with pod leadership this quarter.
To a pod manager
We're not adding a CAB. We want your prompt and model changes to go to 5% of traffic first, with automatic rollback,
and to show up on our dashboards. That means you can ship faster with less risk, and when something does go wrong,
it hits 5% of users for minutes instead of everyone for an hour.

Change standard (one page)
Every production change, including prompts, model versions, safety settings and config:
1. Is versioned and reviewed by a second person.
2. Appears as an annotation on the service's Grafana dashboards.
3. Rolls out gradually (canary at 5% or less first) with automatic rollback on SLI degradation.
4. AI changes pass offline evals against the current version before rollout.
5. Can be rolled back in under 10 minutes; rollback is tested quarterly.
6. During an error-budget freeze: only fixes and reliability changes ship.

How You'll Know It's Working

  • Leading: % of change types annotated; % of prompt and model changes canaried; rollback time tested.
  • Lagging: change-caused incidents falling; change failure rate falling; shorter time to mitigate for change-caused incidents (rollback is quick).

What Actually Happens

"Prompts aren't code."
Counter: show the last quality incident caused by a prompt change. Then ask for the smallest step: annotate every prompt change.
Canaries run too short to catch anything.
Counter: agree a minimum canary duration and traffic level, and let the canary decide automatically on SLIs.
Portal edits bypass everything.
Counter: lock portal permissions down over time, and annotate portal changes from the Activity Log until then.
