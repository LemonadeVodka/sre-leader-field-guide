Implementation Roadmap

Where you are now, what to roll out in which order, and how to run SRE programs when your team doesn't own the code.

How This Section Is Different

SRE Concepts explain what each practice is. This section is how you roll it out as a program you lead: phases, owners, artifacts, how to sell it, and how to know it's working. Every guide is grounded in Google's free Site Reliability Workbook (opens in a new tab), and adapted to a central SRE team that surfaces problems, pods that own the code, an offshore L1 NOC, and an AI product on Azure.

Where Are We Now? A 10-Question Self-Check

Answer yes or no honestly. Count your yeses.

  1. Every critical service has a named owning team and a PagerDuty service.
  2. SRE can see the telemetry (Application Insights, Grafana) for every critical service.
  3. We have at least one written, agreed SLO, and we review it weekly.
  4. Most pages are about user impact, and fewer than 2 pages per on-call shift are noise.
  5. We have written severity criteria that the NOC uses to declare incidents.
  6. Every Sev 1 and Sev 2 gets an RCA within 5 working days, and the actions are tracked to done.
  7. We can roll back a bad change, including prompt and model changes, in minutes.
  8. We know roughly what percentage of the team's time goes to toil and interrupts.
  9. Pods have pre-approved levers that SRE can pull during incidents.
  10. New services go through a readiness check before SRE or the NOC supports them.

The Recommended Order

Each program makes the next one possible. For a team that today mostly surfaces problems, this order works:

WhenProgramWhy nowWhat it unlocks
Quarter 1Ownership, On-Call & EngagementStuck incidents are your biggest pain, and most of the stuck time is "who owns this?"Faster owner engagement; access; pre-approved levers
Quarter 1Incident ProgramSeverity, roles, and comms turn chaos into a process the NOC can runConsistent incidents; good RCAs
Quarter 1–2Observability FoundationYou can't set SLOs or fix alerts without trustworthy telemetrySLIs you can measure; traces for diagnosis
Quarter 2Alerting & PagingNoise is burning out SRE and training the NOC to ignore pagesFewer, better pages; capacity for project work
Quarter 2–3SLOs From ZeroTurns "is it reliable?" into a number you can manage and negotiate withError budgets; data-driven priorities with pods
Quarter 3Safe ChangeMost incidents start with a change, and for AI that includes prompts and modelsFewer change-caused incidents; fast rollback
Quarter 3–4Toil & AutomationWith fewer fires, you can reinvest the hours you get backHeadroom; a lighter load for the NOC

Run at most two programs at once. Put them on the project board you walk in the SRE Daily, and report progress monthly.

How to Run This When You Don't Own the Code

  • Lead with your scope. Incident process, the NOC, alert hygiene, dashboards, and SLO measurement are things SRE can drive mostly on its own.
  • Make the pod asks small and specific. "Add one span attribute" gets done. "Improve observability" doesn't.
  • Use data as leverage. Every program produces numbers (pages, stuck time, budget burn). They're your case to pod managers and leadership.
  • Get leadership to sponsor the program, not just you. One line from your boss to the pod managers ("this is a priority for us this quarter") does more than ten meetings.

What Actually Happens

You try to start all seven programs at once.
Counter: two at a time, maximum. Finish one visibly before starting the next.
Firefighting eats the program time.
Counter: protect a fixed slice of time, such as one engineer's week or 20% of the team. That's how you ever get out of firefighting.
Pods agree in principle and never act.
Counter: get each ask into the pod's own plan with a date, through their manager, and track it like an RCA action.
