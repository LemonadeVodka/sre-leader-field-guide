The Built-In Items

Weekly Ops Review

30 minutes, same time every week, with your SRE leads and, ideally, a rotating pod representative. The goal is to decide things, not to report status.

  • Numbers vs last week (5 min): pages, incidents by severity, hours of unplanned work. Up, down, or flat, and why?
  • Top Offenders (10 min): walk the top 3. Any new ones? Any without an owner? Board
  • Commitments (5 min): anything overdue or moved twice? Decide: help, rescope, or escalate. Overdue view
  • Offshore (5 min): handoff quality, mis-escalations, runbooks that confused people.
  • Decide (5 min): one thing to escalate, one thing to stop doing, one runbook to fix. Write them down as commitments.

Monthly Reliability Review

First Monday of the month. 45 minutes. This is where trends become a story you can take to leadership.

  • Trend: pages, incidents, and hours lost compared with last month and three months ago. Is the climb out working?
  • Repeat incidents: anything that happened twice this month. Did the first review's action items get done?
  • Offenders closed vs opened: if more are opening than closing, say so plainly.
  • The team: on-call load per person, after-hours pages, and anyone showing burnout signs.
  • One escalation: pick the single most important thing leadership needs to act on. Bring data and an ask.
  • Wins: log what improved, with names. Wins Log

Quarterly Planning

  • Re-rate your Skills Map, save a snapshot, and choose next quarter's focus.
  • Copy this quarter's wins as bullets and send them to your boss before review season.
  • Agree with pod leads on the top 3 reliability fixes for next quarter: owner, date, and done means.
  • Write a "not doing this quarter" list and share it.
  • Export a backup of your data.
What Actually Happens
The weekly review gets cancelled whenever there's a fire, which is every week.
Counter: on a fire week, run it for 15 minutes with just Top Offenders and Decide. A shorter review that happens is better than a full one that doesn't.
The monthly review turns into a status slideshow nobody acts on.
Counter: it must end with exactly one escalation with a named decision-maker. If nothing gets decided, the meeting wasn't needed.
You skip the morning scan because Slack already told you everything.
Counter: Slack tells you what's loud, not what's important. The scan exists to catch what nobody shouted about.
